# Fahsa 

Origin: Yemen  
Cooking time: ~90 minutes  
Servings: 2–3



---

## About the Dish

Fahsa is a traditional Yemeni lamb stew, slow-cooked until the meat falls apart,
and served bubbling hot in a stone pot (مرجل, marjal). It is a staple of Yemeni
cuisine, especially popular in Sana'a, and is typically eaten for lunch with
freshly baked Yemeni flatbread (lahoh or khobz).

---

## Ingredients

- 500g bone-in lamb (shoulder or shank), cut into chunks
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 medium tomatoes, diced
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil or ghee
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- 1 tsp ground turmeric
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp hawaij spice mix (or substitute: cardamom + cumin + black pepper)
- Salt to taste
- Fresh coriander (cilantro) for garnish
- 1 green chili (optional - but higly recommended) 

---

## Instructions

1. Brown the meat: Heat the oil or ghee in a heavy pot over medium-high heat.
   Add the lamb chunks and brown on all sides for about 5–7 minutes.

2. Sauté the aromatics: Add the chopped onion and cook until soft and golden,
   about 8 minutes. Add the garlic and cook for another 2 minutes.

3. Add spices and tomatoes: Stir in the cumin, coriander, turmeric, black pepper,
   and hawaij. Cook for 1–2 minutes until fragrant. Add the diced tomatoes and
   tomato paste, stir well.

4. Slow cook: Add just enough water to cover the meat (approx. 400ml).
   Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to low. Cover and simmer for 60–75 minutes,
   until the lamb is very tender and the sauce has thickened.

5. Finish: Taste and adjust salt. The stew should be thick, not watery.
   If too liquid, simmer uncovered for a few more minutes.

6. Serve: Ladle into a deep bowl or stone pot. Garnish with fresh coriander
   and optionally a sliced green chili. Serve immediately with flatbread.

---

## Tips & Tricks

- Fahsa is traditionally served in a *marjal* (a stone or clay pot) that keeps
  it hot throughout the meal — a deep ceramic bowl works as a substitute.
- The dish should be very saucy — use the bread to scoop it up, no cutlery needed.
- Hawaij is a Yemeni spice blend; if unavailable, mix equal parts cardamom,
  cumin, and black pepper as a substitute.
- Leftovers taste even better the next day once the flavors have developed further.
